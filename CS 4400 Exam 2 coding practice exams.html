1. practiceQuery1

Display the first name, salary, and user account of the second male employee who works remotely where the employees are sorted by their last names alphabetically.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery1 as
select e.fname, e.salary, r.user_account
from employee e
join remote_access r on e.ssn = r.ssn
where e.sex = 'M'
order by e.lname
limit 1 offset 1;

Joining employee to remote_access keeps only people who work remotely. Filter to men, sort by last name, and skip one row with LIMIT 1 OFFSET 1. On the original data the remote men are James Borg and Ramesh Narayan, so the second is Narayan.

What the hidden tests change

With department 5 deleted, Narayan disappears and Borg is the only remote man left, so the correct output is empty. A query that falls back to another row fails that state.

2. practiceQuery2

Display the first name, last name, and salary of all employees whose salary is below the company average. Only display the second employee when those employees are sorted by salary in descending order and last name in reverse alphabetical order.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery2 as
select fname, lname, salary
from employee
where salary < (select avg(salary) from employee)

The subquery computes the company average, which is 35,000 on the original data. Sort the employees below it by salary descending, then last name descending, and take the second row. Helene Vincent (32,000) is first and John Smith (30,000) is second.

What the hidden tests change

Deleting departments and changing salaries moves the average in every state, so it has to come from a subquery. Typing 35000 fails four of the five states.

3. practiceQuery3

For every fund source, display the fund source ID, remaining funds, usage rate, customer ID, and customer location, but only display the customer information if the customer has a non-null asset value and the asset value is above the average of all customer assets (excluding null values). Otherwise, display null values for the customer information.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery3 as
select f.fsid, f.remaining, f.usage_rate, c.cid, c.location
from fund_source f
left join customer c
  on c.fsid = f.fsid
 and c.assets is not null
 and c.assets > (select avg(assets) from customer);

Start from fund_source so every fund source stays. The customer conditions go in the LEFT JOIN’s ON clause: when they fail, the customer columns come back NULL instead of the row disappearing. AVG skips NULL assets, which matches “excluding null values.” The average is about 469,333, so only tech3 and bank4 show.

Trap

Putting the asset conditions in WHERE drops 11 of the 13 fund sources, because their customer columns are NULL.

What the hidden tests change

Deleting a department removes its projects, fund sources, and customers, so the average is recomputed from the customers that remain. In the department 4 state, bank1 and bank3 qualify instead.

4. practiceQuery4

Display full name (first_name last_name with space in between), superssn, and salary for each employee who meets either of the following conditions: 1. Is supervised by someone who oversees more than two employees, and the lowest salary among those supervised is greater than 25,000. 2. Is in office, but their room is either unknown or is a whole room (without dash '-' in the room name). Finally, display all the results in alphabetical order by full name.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery4 as
select concat(e.fname, ' ', e.lname) as full_name, e.superssn, e.salary
from employee e
where e.superssn in (select superssn from employee
                     where superssn is not null
                     group by superssn
                     having count(*) > 2 and min(salary) > 25000)
   or e.ssn in (select ssn from in_office
                where room is null or room not like '%-%')
order by full_name;

Condition 1 groups employees by supervisor and keeps supervisors with more than two people whose lowest salary is over 25,000. Condition 2 checks in_office for a NULL room or a room with no dash. OR combines them.

  • James Borg supervises three people with a minimum salary of 37,000, so Emily Davis, Franklin Wong, and Jennifer Wallace qualify.
  • Jennifer Wallace’s team has a minimum of exactly 25,000, which isn’t greater than 25,000. Ahmad Jabbar and Alicia Zelaya still appear through condition 2: room Bridge and a NULL room.
  • Franklin Wong also meets condition 2 (room 100), but each employee appears once because the query reads employee rows.

What the hidden tests change

The new department 9 adds a supervisor, 235711131, with four employees who all earn more than 25,000, so they all join the result.

5. practiceQuery5

For each project that has at least one fund source, display the project name, the project’s number of fund sources whose usage rate is below 3,000, and the highest budget balance among those fund sources. Include projects even if their fund sources have no budget (that is, a NULL balance). Sort the results from highest number of fund sources, then by project name alphabetically, then show only the first five projects.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery5 as
select p.pname, count(f.fsid) as num_fund_sources,
       max(b.balance) as max_balance
from project p
join fund_source f on f.pnumber = p.pnumber
left join budget b on b.fsid = f.fsid
where f.usage_rate < 3000
group by p.pnumber, p.pname
order by num_fund_sources desc, p.pname
limit 5;

Inner-join project to fund_source, since a project needs a fund source, and keep usage_rate below 3,000 in WHERE. LEFT JOIN budget so fund sources without a budget still count. MAX(balance) is NULL when none of them has a budget, which is why Computerization shows NULL.

What the hidden tests change

In the department 5 state, only Computerization and Newbenefits qualify, so LIMIT 5 returns two rows. Reorganization still has a fund source there, but its usage rate is exactly 3,000, so it must not appear. That confirms the filter belongs in WHERE: moving it into a LEFT JOIN would list Reorganization with a count of 0.

2. practiceQuery2

Display the employee SSN, full name (formatted as “First Name, Last Name”), and salary for employees whose department number is greater than or equal to the maximum department number among projects located in Bellaire or Houston.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery2 as
select ssn, concat(fname, ', ', lname) as full_name, salary
from employee
where dno >= (select max(dnum) from project
              where plocation in ('Bellaire', 'Houston'));

The subquery finds the largest dnum among Bellaire or Houston projects, which is 6 on the original data. Compare dno against it with >=. CONCAT builds the “First, Last” format. Because the grader runs with PIPES_AS_CONCAT, fname || ', ' || lname works too.

What the hidden tests change

  • Projects move between cities and employees change departments, which moves the maximum.
  • When projects 1, 3, 50, and 53 move to Sugarland, the maximum drops to 1 and every employee qualifies.
  • When no project is in Bellaire or Houston, the subquery returns NULL, the comparison is never true, and the correct output is empty.

3. practiceQuery3

With SSNs arranged in decreasing order, display the 2nd to 5th employee SSNs for employees who either work in the Security building or in any office room that ends with an 'A' (for example 1-A or 3A), or have only logged in for remote sessions with time frame durations greater than 4 hours.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery3 as
select e.ssn
from employee e
left join in_office o on e.ssn = o.ssn
where o.building = 'Security'
   or o.room like '%A'
   or e.ssn in (select ssn from time_frames
                group by ssn
                having min(duration) > 4)
order by e.ssn desc
limit 4 offset 1;

Three conditions joined with OR. “Only logged in for sessions longer than 4 hours” means every session is longer than 4 hours, so group time_frames by ssn and keep MIN(duration) > 4. Sort SSNs descending, skip the first, and keep four: LIMIT 4 OFFSET 1.

On the original data six employees qualify. 712039854 is first, so it’s skipped, and 111223333 is sixth, so it’s cut.

  • Rooms are renamed 5A and A5 in the Research building. Only 5A ends with A.
  • Everyone moves to the Security building.
  • Every session gets 3 hours longer.
  • Durations drop below 5 and rooms become '1-A-1' in 'The Security building'. Nothing qualifies, so the correct output is empty.

4. practiceQuery4

For each department, display the department name, its total number of budgets, and the balance range (calculated as the maximum balance minus the minimum balance) among all of its budget records. Only show departments that have both a balance range exceeding $40,000 and at least one budget with a balance that meets or exceeds the maximum balance within department 6.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery4 as
select d.dname, count(*) as num_budgets,
       max(b.balance) - min(b.balance) as balance_range
from department d
join budget b on d.dnumber = b.dnumber
group by d.dnumber, d.dname
having max(b.balance) - min(b.balance) > 40000
   and max(b.balance) >= (select max(balance) from budget where dnumber = 6);

Group budgets by department. HAVING keeps groups whose range is strictly above 40,000 and whose largest balance is at least department 6’s largest. If a department’s maximum meets that bar, at least one of its budgets does, so comparing MAX is enough.

Department 6 (Security) has one budget of 12,000, so its own range is 0 and it drops out.

What the hidden tests change

  • New department 6 budgets raise its maximum, so Security can qualify and Headquarters can drop out.
  • Two tests set the range to exactly 40,001 and exactly 40,000. Only the first passes “exceeding,” so the comparison must be >, not >=.

5. practiceQuery5

For each department, display the department number, average salary of the female employees in that department (rounded to 2 decimal places), and the total hours worked in that department by the female employees. Only consider employees that recorded the number of hours they worked on the project. Only show departments with more than 50 total hours worked by their female employees.

Solution

create or replace view practiceQuery5 as
select e.dno, round(avg(e.salary), 2) as avg_female_salary,
       sum(w.hours) as total_hours
from employee e
join works_on w on e.ssn = w.essn
where e.sex = 'F' and w.hours is not null
group by e.dno
having sum(w.hours) > 50;

Join employee to works_on so you can sum the hours. WHERE keeps women and non-NULL hours, GROUP BY makes one row per department, and HAVING keeps totals above 50.

Trap

AVG runs over the joined rows, so a woman on four projects counts four times. The expected answer for department 4 is 36,888.89, the join-weighted average. Averaging each woman once gives 34,250 and loses the points.

What the hidden tests change

  • John Smith’s sex changes to F, which adds his hours and salary to department 5.
  • Joyce English’s hours drop to 5 per project, so department 5 totals exactly 50 and drops out.
  • Emily Davis moves to department 6 with 31 hours, so department 6 totals 51 and appears.
  • Jennifer Wallace’s hours become NULL, so her rows leave both the sum and the average.

Output on the original data

dnoavg_female_salarytotal_hours

527333.3380.0
